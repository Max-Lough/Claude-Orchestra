# Critique

## Steelman

The rival plan replaces runtime-specific agents with capability contracts, a one-primary-per-class taxonomy, dispatch-time casting, provenance-aware review, deterministic evidence, and pool-aware degradation. It combines nuanced role boundaries with explicit migration, rollback, allowance, qualification, and liveness mechanisms. Its strongest contribution is treating cross-family review as an enforceable operational system rather than a naming convention.

## Findings

1. **[BLOCKER] Part 2, Role 3 “Synthesizer,” contradicts P5 and fails the self-referential check.** Contest extraction is described as mechanical, but deciding whether two plans disagree, merely use different terminology, or share an omitted premise is semantic adjudication. Each “opposite-family” challenger also belongs to the family holding the competing position, while the family-aligned Synthesizer controls contest extraction and final composition without independent review. This permits biased framing, omitted contests, or selective composition to survive and institutionalizes the same family-correlated merge risk the protocol claims to prevent.

2. **[BLOCKER] Role 21’s reviewer matrix cannot guarantee independence for “Human / unattributed” artifacts.** It maps both categories to Opus, even though an unattributed artifact may have been produced or materially edited by Anthropic models. The order, report, and verdict contracts do not define a signed author-family or complete co-author-family set sufficient to prove otherwise. Missing provenance must fail closed or route to a human/third family; the current rule can issue a same-family closing verdict while reporting compliance with the hard invariant.

3. **[MAJOR] Role 23 “Verifier” is not actually the deterministic substrate promised by P6.** It permits cheapest-tier model assistance and ensemble votes, but its output schema does not record whether assistance affected `PASS`, the assisting family, or the deterministic rule that absorbs a wrong vote. If model votes influence the outcome, model judgment is being presented as de-correlated deterministic evidence; if they cannot influence it, their purpose is undefined. This invites an incorrect or same-family-assisted `PASS` to enter a deterministic-only closure path.

4. **[MAJOR] Role 15 “Interface Artisan” creates an untracked author/reviewer transition.** The Opus “closing casting” verifies behavior and code and “closes,” yet the role retains `WRITE-TREE` and is not converted into a distinct read-only R0 order. If that pass edits the implementation, Opus becomes a co-author and can no longer close it; if it cannot edit, the plan should say so and distinguish its verdict from the subsequent code Reviewer. The ambiguity invites self-review, incomplete provenance, or an expensive three-pass process whose authorities overlap.

5. **[MAJOR] Role 21 and WO-12 use underpowered reviewer qualifications.** With only 20 major seeded defects, one observation equals five percentage points; even 19/20 recall has a wide 95% interval, roughly 76–99%. The final plan dropped the supplied own plan’s requirement to report confidence intervals, yet allows Terra or Gemini to close consequential work based on “within 5 points” qualification. Likewise, M0’s 20-task “within 3 points” rule is not measurable from binary task outcomes without defining a continuous score. These gates can qualify a weaker lane on sampling noise.

6. **[MAJOR] Part 5.3 does not expose the arithmetic behind its load-bearing capacity conclusions.** The illustrative mix specifies only about 75 of 100 primary orders and leaves the remaining classes and risk distribution as “the rest smaller,” while still asserting approximately 48 reviews, 18 Q0 orders, and a 60% OpenAI review share. Unlike the supplied own plan, it omits the complete class-frequency and within-pool share tables needed to reproduce those results. The later claim that two pools last “roughly twice as long” also ignores unequal pool sizes and the admitted OpenAI bottleneck; it can drive reserve sizing and deliberate author recasting from unverifiable arithmetic.

7. **[MAJOR] Parts 3.4 and 7.2 specify mutually inconsistent liveness thresholds.** Capacity of 1.3× peak arrival implies 76.9% utilization at peak, while the plan simultaneously requires utilization at or below 70%, which needs at least 1.43× capacity. WO-2 stops only below 1.3×, so a deployment between those bounds passes the early stop condition but is guaranteed to fail the whole-system gate after substantial implementation and shadow work.

8. **[MAJOR] Role 6 “Long-Context Analyst” uses a stale blanket limit for its Anthropic mirror.** It restricts Opus 5 to corpora of at most 200K tokens, but current Anthropic documentation lists a 1M Claude Code context window for Opus 5, Sonnet 5, and Fable 5 on paid plans, with a specific usage-credit caveat for Pro users using Opus—not a universal 200K ceiling. [Anthropic context-window documentation](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8606394-how-large-is-the-context-window-on-paid-claude-plans) On the Max/premium configuration already assumed elsewhere, this unnecessarily favors Terra and causes avoidable sharding or waiting.

9. **[MAJOR] Part 4.1’s residual routing rule defeats both class uniqueness and the cost model.** When two primaries still match, it routes to “the cheaper of the two,” even though Part 5 explicitly says AU, OU, and GU do not convert. Ambiguity is therefore resolved by an undefined comparison rather than acceptance risk or a corrected discriminator. This can under-cast work while falsely preserving the appearance of exactly-one-primary coverage.

10. **[MINOR] WO-4 through WO-7 validate the taxonomy after implementing it.** The plan encodes all 24 classes and builds the router in WO-4/WO-6, then runs the classification corpus in WO-7 and says failed boundaries should be redrawn “before” agent definitions exist. Running the corpus before registry and router implementation would test the most failure-prone assumption sooner and avoid reworking schemas, routing tables, and policy tests.

11. **[MAJOR] The Done-criteria’s evidence-provenance promise is not met.** The plan contains many load-bearing external figures but no evidence register or direct citation map for claims such as the Luna-max cost/quality ratio, the orchestration study, prose-panel results, allowance-policy oscillation, or silent model substitution. Several prominent claims can currently be confirmed from primary sources—such as [Codex allowance ranges](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/pricing), [Fable subscription mechanics](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/15424964-claude-fable-5-on-your-plan), [Antigravity model availability](https://antigravity.google/docs/models/), and [Terminal-Bench results](https://www.frontierbench.ai/)—but the document itself does not make decisions traceable or refreshable. The stale N2 context rule demonstrates the resulting failure mode.

## Comparative assessment

The rival plan is stronger than the supplied own plan in its capability-contract/casting separation, mirror-or-declared-exception discipline, per-bucket Quartermaster, explicit Refactorer and independent Sweeper, writable disposable verification checkout, authorization packet, and migration safety through aliases, canaries, and a per-order kill switch. Its mandatory/preferred review bands also make the safety-versus-liveness tradeoff more operationally realistic than a universal non-degradable mandate. Repository checks support several of its motivating observations, including the current Detective’s execution prohibition, the existing terminal sweep, and the review runner’s need for a writable test checkout.

The supplied own plan is stronger in keeping the Verifier strictly non-model, publishing a complete workload mix and reproducible pool-share calculations, testing the classification corpus before committing to the registry, retaining a smaller taxonomy, and requiring continuing requalification after provider changes. Most importantly, its third-family-or-human synthesis rule is safer than the rival’s pointwise challenge protocol.

The routing table’s nominal defaults comprise 11 Anthropic primaries, 8 OpenAI primaries, 1 optional Google primary, and 2 provenance-dependent roles. That imbalance alone does not establish catalog-wide vendor bias: Anthropic is concentrated in planning and difficult implementation, while OpenAI owns several high-volume specialties and bears the forecast review bottleneck. The asymmetries also point in opposing directions—A1 concentrates merge control on Anthropic, the incorrect N2 limit favors OpenAI, and mandatory review of every Sol-authored mutation uniquely penalizes OpenAI. The evidence therefore supports a material local adjudication skew in A1, but not a coherent vendor-wide bias. Overall, the merge is broader and operationally richer than either source in several areas, but it is not strictly stronger than both because it lost deterministic boundaries, reproducible cost evidence, early taxonomy validation, and the safer synthesis rule.
